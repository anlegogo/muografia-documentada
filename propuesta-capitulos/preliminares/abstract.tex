\textbf{Title:} Deep learning-assisted transmission muographic reconstruction through predictive enhancement of low-statistics images for volcanic exploration.\footnote{Undergraduate thesis, research modality.}

\textbf{Authors:} \AutorUno; \AutorDos.\footnote{Universidad Industrial de Santander. Faculty of Physical and Mechanical Engineering. School of Systems Engineering and Informatics. Supervisor: \DirectorNombre, PhD candidate. Co-supervisor: \CodirectorNombre, PhD.}

\textbf{Keywords:} transmission muography, volcanic exploration, deep learning, Monte Carlo simulation, image enhancement, low statistics.

\textbf{Description:}

% Faithful translation of the Spanish description; keep both consistent.
Transmission muography estimates the density distribution inside a volcano from the attenuation of the cosmic-ray muon flux, but the low natural flux requires exposures of weeks or months before density contrasts can be distinguished from counting noise. This work developed and evaluated a scheme, based on deep learning and trained with synthetic data, to enhance low-statistics muographic images. Monte Carlo simulations with Geant4, with a muon source derived from a flux parametrization and the EcoMug generator, produced 114 reference images of nineteen idealized volcanic scenarios and, through binomial thinning, 9,120 input--reference pairs with 50, 10, 5 and 1\,\% of the counts, partitioned by geometry. A conditional Wasserstein generative adversarial network with gradient penalty, composed of a U-Net generator and a PatchGAN critic, was designed, trained and evaluated with 5\,\% of the counts, together with a U-Net trained with an L1 loss and variants tuned to conserve the counts. In the final evaluation, a descriptive evaluation on potentially exposed data because the reserved scenes had been evaluated before, the generative network increased the test SSIM from 0.7643 to 0.8342 and the PSNR from 24.258 to 31.170~dB with respect to its input and, according to both metrics, outperformed the unprocessed image with twice the counts in each of the four reserved geometries, although not the one with ten times more counts. At the same time, it increased the regional count bias from 1.832 to 14.566\,\%, and the variants that reduced this bias lowered the similarity. Image quality comparable to that of high statistics was therefore only partially supported. When later evaluated with 1, 5, 10 and 50\,\% of the counts, none of the models met the adopted criterion against the unprocessed input at 50\,\%, so no reduction factor of the acquisition time was demonstrated; the nominal value of 20 describes only the count reduction of the input. The results are limited to idealized synthetic scenarios and to models trained with a single count fraction, and the anomaly indicators, computed in regions of known location, do not demonstrate the detection of unknown anomalies.
